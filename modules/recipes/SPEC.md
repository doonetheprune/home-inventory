# Recipes

Recipes the household liked, saved so they can be found again and checked against what's in the house. Larder doesn't invent recipes: AI assistants do, from what's in stock and what needs using soon (the `expiring` and `search` tools), and save the good ones here.

- **Data:** `recipes` collection: `{ id, title, serves, ingredients: [{ name, productId, quantity, amount, optional, ratio, ratioUnit }], method, notes, source, lastCookedAt, timesCooked, presets: [{ name, times }], defaultPreset, scenarioIds, scenarioOnly }`. `quantity` is in what the linked item is bought in (1 tin, 50 g of cheese bought loose) and is what cooking takes from stock; `amount` is how the recipe puts it ("400 g", "a handful"). When the upgrade to 0.10.0's units rescales an item (`product.recounted`: kg became g, or tea bags became boxes containing 20), its ingredients' `quantity` is multiplied by the factor to match. Ingredients are linked to items when their name (or `item`) clearly is one, using the core's strict name matching; unclear ones stay unlinked rather than guessed. Saving again keeps an ingredient's link unless it's given a new `item` or `productId` (`null` unlinks it); `serves: null` clears it, leaving it out keeps it. Assistants find recipes by title with the same strict rules (`core.resolve.among`). Recipes saved before ratios, presets or scenarios read as having none.
- **Ratios:** `ratio` is how much of an ingredient goes in for the recipe as saved, relative to the others, in `ratioUnit`: eggs 2 (a count, no unit), plain flour 100 `g`, milk 300 `ml`. A ratio like 3 : 2 : 1 is saved as a batch (300 g, 200 g, 100 g). Units are stored the same way however they're typed ("grams" → `g`, "slices" → `slice`). Scaling converts between g, kg, oz and lb, and between ml, cl, L, pints (UK), tsp (5 ml), tbsp (15 ml) and fl oz. g, kg, ml, cl, L and pints are the core's units, with its spellings; `units.ts` only adds the kitchen measures, each so many of the core's g or ml. Any other word is a count that only matches itself, with its singular and plural from the core's unit list. A plain count matches an item bought in or containing the same thing (2 eggs and an item bought in eggs, or in boxes containing 6 eggs), never anything else (8 chicken thighs aren't 8 packs). An ingredient with no ratio scales by its stock `quantity` if it's linked; otherwise it stays as written, marked "not scaled".
- **Scaling** (`ScaleInput`): one of `preset` (by name, same strict rules), `servings` (needs `serves`), `times` (2 doubles it; 1 is the amounts as saved) or `have: [{ ingredient, amount, unit? }]`, how much there is of some ingredients. From `have`, each ingredient sets how many times the recipe it allows, and the smallest wins, so nothing needs more than there is ("limited by the plain flour"). Nothing given means the default preset, or the amounts as saved. The view (`RecipeView`) gives each ingredient `shown` (the amount at that scale, e.g. "67 g"), `label` ("67 g plain flour", "1 egg"), `scales`, and `need` (what cooking takes from stock at that scale); `status` and `missing` use `need`. `scale` says how it was worked out.
- **Stock from ratios:** with no `quantity`, cooking takes the ratio from stock through the core's converter (`toBoughtIn`): 500 ml of milk bought in bottles containing 4 pints is 0.22 bottle (through its Weight, 2273 ml), and 2 eggs of boxes containing 6 is 0.333 box. When the converter can't (an item bought in jars with no Weight), the ingredient isn't taken, and the view says why (`why`, the converter's sentence); a count the item isn't bought in or containing is left out without one.
- **Presets:** up to 20 per recipe, each a name and how many times the recipe as saved it is (`times`, kept to 4 decimal places). `defaultPreset` names the one used when no amount is asked for; none means the amounts as saved. Saving a preset under an existing name (any case) replaces its amount and keeps its name; forgetting the default goes back to the amounts as saved. Editing the recipe keeps its presets unless `presets` is given.
- **Cooking:** `POST /:id/cook` (body: `ScaleInput`, default preset when empty) proposes what to take out (scaled, never more than there is) and **changes nothing**. `POST /proposals/:proposalId/confirm` `{ items?: [{ productId, quantity }] }` applies it, with any quantities changed (0 leaves an item alone), all together or not at all (`core.stock.useMany`). Two lines for the same item add up to one. Changes can only adjust items in the proposal. Proposals last six hours and work once, even when confirmed twice at the same moment.
- **Scenarios:** a recipe can belong to scenarios (`scenarioIds`, from the scenarios module) and be kept for them only (`scenarioOnly`), so a Halloween recipe isn't on the main list at Christmas. Recipes keep only the ids and ask the scenarios module (its `GET /`) for the current ones: when a scenario is removed or the module is switched off, it stops counting, and a scenario-only recipe is back on the main list. Saving refuses scenarios that don't exist, and `scenarioOnly` without any. Views carry `scenarios` (`[{ id, name }]`, current ones) and `hidden` (kept for its scenarios).
- **Other routes** (under `/api/v1/modules/recipes`): `GET /` (the main list; `?scenarioId=` a scenario's recipes, `?productId=` every recipe using an item, `?show=main|scenario-only|all`), `POST /`, `GET /:id`, `PUT /:id`, `DELETE /:id`, `POST /:id/scale` (body: `ScaleInput`), `POST /:id/presets` (`{ name, default?, times? | servings? | have? }`; with only `name` and `default`, it changes the default), `DELETE /:id/presets/:name`, `POST /:id/missing-to-list` (body: `ScaleInput`, optional). The list, a recipe and search use each recipe's default preset. Changes to a saved recipe go one at a time.
- **Search:** "Saved recipes" (order 70), by title and ingredients, saying what's missing.
- **AI tools:** `save_recipe` (new, or changing one given as `recipe`: ingredients left out are dropped, and fields an ingredient leaves out stay as they were; with `scenarios` by name and `scenario_only`), `list_recipes` (`scenario` filter; scenario recipes say "only for Christmas"), `get_recipe` (with `preset`, `have`, `servings` or `times`), `save_recipe_preset`, `forget_recipe_preset`, `propose_cooking` (same amounts as `get_recipe`), `confirm_cooking`, `set_recipe_scenarios`, `recipe_to_shopping_list`, `forget_recipe`. `get_recipe` lists the presets and says which is the default; with presets but no default it tells the assistant to ask which one. After working out amounts from what they have, servings or times, it suggests offering to save them as a preset. The assistant is told to show a cooking proposal and ask before confirming.
- **Demo:** three recipes in the example household; Pancakes has ratios and two presets ("Just me", "Family"), and its milk has no `quantity`, so 300 ml is taken from bottles containing 4 pints by the converter.
- **UI:** the recipe list, with scenario-only recipes folded away under "Kept for scenarios"; a recipe page with each ingredient in or not (a linked one opens its item), amounts by preset ("As saved", each preset) or **I've got…** (one ingredient's amount), **Save as preset**, **Cooked it** at the amounts shown (adjust, then confirm) and **Add missing to list**; an editor with each ingredient's ratio and unit, the presets with their default, scenario chips and "Only show in these scenarios"; a **Recipes** panel on item pages (every recipe using it); and a **Recipes** panel on scenario pages (slot `scenarios.scenario`), whose "Add one" starts a recipe in that scenario, shown only there.
- **Offline copies:** both lists, every recipe, and the recipe lists on the item and scenario pages that have any are saved in a phone's offline copy (`ctx.addOfflinePaths`), and the pages are shown while a copy is open (`offline: true`). Scaling, cooking and changes need the live Larder.
